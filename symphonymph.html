Symphonymph Free Audio

Podcasts, audiobooks, internet radio, lectures and independent music, from the world's open archives. Free to listen. No account to make. Never an ad from us.
Android. In testing now — coming to Google Play.

 Android first, in active development. Sign up below to hear when it launches.

Why We Built It

Listening got expensive. The paid apps cost more every year, and the free ones make you the product — full-screen ads between tracks, countdowns you have to sit through, buttons you have to hunt for to close. We wanted an app that simply plays audio, and stops there.

It turns out there is an enormous amount of audio that is already free and legal to listen to. Libraries, universities, archives, broadcasters and independent artists have been publishing openly for decades. The problem was never supply. It was that nobody had put it all in one place with a decent player in front of it.

That is Symphonymph: millions of recordings from the world's open catalogs, in one app, with nothing between you and the play button.

What's Inside

Audiobooks & Poetry

Tens of thousands of complete audiobooks read by volunteers, plus poetry and spoken-word collections — the classics, in full, free because the works themselves are in the public domain. Resume where you left off, download for offline, listen at your own pace.

Podcasts

An open, independent podcast directory — not one company's walled catalog. Shows are ranked by what people are actually listening to this week, and every episode streams from the publisher's own feed, exactly as any podcast app does.

Internet Radio

Live stations from nearly every country on earth, from community broadcasters to national services. Filter by language so you hear the ones you can actually understand — or leave it open and go exploring.

Independent Music

Artist-distributed tracks, Creative Commons releases from netlabels, and a vast archive of live concert recordings from bands who explicitly allow taping and sharing. Hundreds of thousands of shows, free and legal.

Lectures & Talks

University lecture recordings, conference talks and educational series — the kind of material that used to sit behind a campus login. Sorted alongside everything else, so learning is one tap from listening.

Radio Drama & More

Classic old-time radio serials, news, meditation and sleep audio, religion and spirituality, and material in dozens of languages. New categories are added as we verify new archives.

Sound Effects & Field Recordings

Rain on a tent, a crowd in a stadium, a door in a hallway — hundreds of thousands of openly licensed recordings from Freesound and Wikimedia Commons, given their own shelf. For sound designers, teachers, and anyone who sleeps better to an hour of weather.

Follow a Show

Follow a podcast and its new episodes appear at the top of Home, with the unread ones marked. Your subscriptions are yours: import them from any other podcast app as OPML, and export them the same way, whenever you like. Nothing is locked in.

Transcripts

Where a publisher provides one, read the episode as you listen: the line being spoken is highlighted, and tapping any line jumps the audio there. Built for Deaf and hard-of-hearing listeners first, and useful to everyone who has ever wanted to find "the bit about…".

Share, and Credit the Source

Sharing sends people to the recording's own page at its archive or publisher — never to a bare audio file — with the licence and the creator named. The source gets the visit; Symphonymph is mentioned last.

What the App Actually Does

Everything on this page is built and working in the version going to Google Play. Nothing here is a plan.

A player that gets out of the way

Background playback that survives locking the phone and swiping the app away. Lock-screen and notification controls, with the pair of buttons beside play chosen by you. Bluetooth and headset buttons. A visible queue you can reorder, shuffle and re-deal. Playback speed, remembered per show or per book. Skip silence. A ten-band equalizer and a loudness boost for quiet archive recordings.

Downloads and offline listening

Download anything whose license allows it and play it with no signal at all. Downloads resume after a dropped connection. Follow a show and new episodes can download themselves — on Wi-Fi only, if you prefer. A data saver asks for smaller streams where a catalog offers them.

Keeping your place, and your library

Every recording remembers where you stopped. Named bookmarks for the passages you want to find again. Playlists, collections, a Liked list, full listening history, and a running total of how long you have actually listened. Back the whole library up to a file and restore it — on this phone or the next one.

Yours to arrange

Drag the menu into the order you want. Choose which shelves appear on Home, and in what order. Hide whole categories you never open. Nine accent colors, light or dark or follow the system — and the app icon on your home screen changes color with it.

Finding things

Search every catalog at once, with filters that combine — podcasts and comedy, audiobooks and history. Speak instead of typing. Paste a podcast address and follow it directly. “More like this” from anything playing, and everything else by the same artist. Recent searches, kept on the device.

Beyond the phone

Android Auto, so your library is browsable from the car. Google Cast to a speaker or TV, with the queue handed over. A Quick Settings tile for play and pause without unlocking. Your own music files, too — “On this device” plays what is already on the phone, and never moves or deletes a file.

Filters you control

Hide anything a publisher marked explicit. Limit what you are offered to the languages you read. Inside Religion & spirituality, choose which faiths appear at all and drag your own to the front. A sleep timer that can stop after a set time or at the end of what is playing.

Nothing locked in

No account is needed to press play. No analytics, no tracking, no crash reporter, no advertising identifier — nothing about your listening leaves the phone. Import your subscriptions from any other podcast app as OPML, and export them the same way whenever you like.

Artists: Share Your Work

If you make audio — music, a podcast, spoken word, field recordings, anything — and you would like it available in Symphonymph, send us a link. We will review it and, if it fits, index it so listeners can find it.

Send a link, not a file. Symphonymph never hosts audio, so your work stays exactly where you already put it — your own site, your podcast feed, Bandcamp, ccMixter, Audius, or anywhere else that serves the audio publicly. You keep ownership and you keep control: take it down at your end and it disappears from the app.

No host of your own? The Internet Archive will store your audio free and permanently, for anyone, and Symphonymph already reads it. Upload there, then paste the link below.

To be clear about money: Symphonymph pays nothing, because Symphonymph earns nothing. There is no ad revenue to split, and no paid tier yet. What we can do is give your work an audience and link every listener straight to your own page — the one you host it on, where your donation or purchase link already lives — so anything a listener chooses to give goes to you in full.

Submissions are reviewed by hand, by one person, so please allow time. Because Symphonymph reads the open catalogues directly, the fastest route in is often to publish on one of them: an Internet Archive upload, a ccMixter or Audius release, or a podcast feed listed in the Podcast Index appears in the app on its own, with no form needed.

Keeping It Free

Symphonymph runs on donations and on the goodwill of the archives that make it possible. The catalogs behind the app — libraries, universities, volunteer directories and nonprofit archives — are the real work. If you have something to give, give it to them first; we will always tell you who they are.

One thing will ever carry a price: the account that carries your library to every device you own. One payment, once. Never a subscription, and never required.
