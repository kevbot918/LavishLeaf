# The store: every product ranked, in five tiers and a miscellany

**Rebuilt 2026-09-28 (the day pass, second edition), on the measurements of
09-20, 09-27 and both passes of 09-28.** This is the one store document. Every
product row carries a link to that product on the supplier's own page, and
every link was opened in a browser during one of those passes; none was
constructed from a pattern. A price is a measurement with a date.

**How the tiers are ordered in this edition.** Within each tier the products
are **ranked, first to last, by how strongly they are recommended for the
store**: rank 1 is the line to stock first. The ranking weighs, in this order,
whether it fits his brief (non-liquid, garden, kitchen, games, soap), what it
costs to post (weight), whether it is bought again, whether the price is
public, how low the opening minimum is, and the trade signal (rating count,
country of origin). A rank is a recommendation, not a measurement, and the
reason is in the last column.

> His brief, in his words: *"Tier 1 products: garden fertilizers, pesticides,
> cleaning supplies (emphasis on eco-friendly, non liquids/concentrates),
> natural utensils and kitchenware, some of those family/kid games, soaps.
> Tier 2 could be items like garden tools, household items to replace plastic
> and such. Natural clothes/towels. More expensive items that I would have to
> store at home. Tier 3: is up to you and your research."* Plus: *"I do want
> the heavy DTE fertilizers at some point, maybe just to buy in person, so
> include a Tier 4 for Retail Store items"*, a **Tier 5** of everything worth a
> look later, and: *"Any products that may not fit in a tier then include a
> miscellaneous tier."*

## 0. How to read this

| Tier | What it is | Where it sells |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The opening online order: light, no registration of his own, bought again | Online |
| **2** | The second round: heavier, dearer or slower, still postable | Online |
| **3** | The third round: gifts, collectables, serving pieces, seed gifts, bundles | Online |
| **4** | **Heavy, bulk and perishable**: the retail store, the stall, pickup, the supplier's truck | **Never posted** |
| **5** | The long list: everything researched that may fit later | Later |
| **Misc** | Real, read, priced, and in no tier: fixtures, commercial sizes, ingredients, impulse lines | Case by case |

**Price kinds, never mixed in a column.** **WHOLESALE**: a real case or unit
price from the supplier's page (Bangalla, Hummert, WebstaurantStore, Seven
Springs by the sack). **RETAIL**: the supplier's shelf price, the ceiling and
not his cost (Arbico, GrowOrganic, Frontier, Botanical Interests, Neptune's
Harvest, eeBoo, Dr. Earth, Seven Springs). **MSRP**: what Faire shows a
signed-out visitor; the wholesale price is behind the account, the opening
minimum is public ("no minimum shown" means the page printed none).
**LISTED**: Azure's member price. **HIDDEN**: needs an approved account.

## 1. The decisions that shape every list

**The number that decides a category is the shipping weight, not the margin.**

| | Retail | Typical margin | Ship weight | Margin after a $9 parcel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 lb box of blood meal | $18 | $7 | 5 lb | **under $1** |
| Bamboo toothbrush 4-pack | $12 | $6 | 4 oz | **$5** |
| 32 oz laundry powder | $17 | $7 | 2 lb | **$3** |
| Compost tumbler | $120 | $40 | 35 lb | **negative** unless the supplier ships it |

That is why Tier 4 exists, and why the rankings below put a $7 cloth above a
$20 sack. Seven settled decisions: **non-liquid first**; **a pesticide claim
is a registration** (the manufacturer's, $210 a label in Oklahoma; copy the
label and add nothing); **fertiliser is the same shape** ($100 a product, the
manufacturer's; the $50 licence is his); **nothing from China by parcel** (a
Faire page's "Made in: China" is the maker's import, already landed; it is
noted so he can weigh it); **the FTC thirty-day rule applies**; **check stock
before a product gets a page**; **a supplier's price list is not its shipping
list** (three DTE products cannot enter Oklahoma from Arbico).

## A. The regulatory ground, in full

The five decisions in §1 are the short form of this. Read A before
writing a single product description, and read it again before writing one
for anything that kills a bug or feeds a plant.

This section was researched independently of the three suppliers because it applies to all of them, and because two whole product categories the owner asked for (organic fertilizers and amendments, and pesticides) are regulated product classes in Oklahoma, not ordinary merchandise. None of this is legal advice. It is a list of the rules that exist and where to read them.

#### 1.1 The base requirement: sales tax permit and resale certificate

All three suppliers will want a resale certificate. In Oklahoma a resale certificate can only be issued by a business that already holds a valid Oklahoma Sales Tax Permit, which is applied for through the Oklahoma Tax Commission for a fee in the region of twenty dollars plus handling. Oklahoma accepts the Multistate Tax Commission Uniform Sales and Use Tax Certificate, which is convenient because the same form can be handed to an out of state supplier such as Arbico in Arizona. Suppliers generally want the certificate within ninety days of the sale, and the records should be kept for at least three years.

Practical order of operations: get the sales tax permit first, then the resale certificate, then apply to suppliers. Applying to Bangalla without an EIN and a resale certificate will stall.

Sources: [Oklahoma business licenses and permits](https://oklahoma.gov/business/operate/licenses-and-permits.html), [Oklahoma resale certificate guide](https://taxhero.net/blog/resale-certificate-oklahoma/), [Oklahoma sales tax permit registration](https://handsoffsalestax.com/sales-tax-permit-oklahoma/).

#### 1.2 Pesticides: the rule that catches people out

This is the single most important compliance finding in this report, and it is stronger than the brief assumed.

**Every pesticide sold into Oklahoma must be registered with the State Board of Agriculture.** The Oklahoma pesticide law says, in the section headed PESTICIDE REGISTRATION REQUIRED: "Every pesticide or device distributed, sold, or offered for sale within this state or delivered for transportation or transported in intrastate or interstate commerce shall be registered with the Board." The annual fee is "Two Hundred Ten Dollars ($210.00) for each pesticide or device label registered."

**Who pays that is the good news.** The rules say: "Unless provided otherwise, registrants shall be responsible for the registration of pesticides and devices and may be sanctioned by the Department if unregistered pesticides or devices are distributed, sold or offered for sale within this state ... by registrants, distributors or dealers." The registrant is the company whose name is on the label, that is Monterey, Bonide, Safer, Neudorff and so on. **A retailer does not file the registration and does not pay the $210.** What a retailer must do is make sure the product it lists is in fact registered in Oklahoma, because the rule expressly contemplates the Department sanctioning distributors and dealers who sell unregistered product.

**Three traps worth knowing:**

1. **"Minimum risk" does not mean exempt in Oklahoma.** Many natural pest products (essential oil sprays, some soaps and garlic or rosemary oil products) are exempt from federal EPA registration under FIFRA section 25(b). Oklahoma does not follow that exemption. The rules state: "Pesticides and devices classified under sections 24(c) or 25(b) of the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act, 7 U.S.C. 136 et seq. shall be registered with the Department and shall comply with all registration requirements established by the Department." So the friendly looking "EPA exempt" natural bug spray still needs an Oklahoma registration behind it. This matches the general national picture, where most states require registration or notification for 25(b) products despite the federal exemption.

2. **"Devices" are regulated too.** Oklahoma defines a device as "any instrument subject to the United States Environmental Protection Agency regulation intended for trapping, destroying, repelling, or mitigating insects or rodents, or mitigating fungi, bacteria, or weeds." That language reaches sticky traps, pheromone traps and similar monitoring products, at the same $210 per label. Any trap listed in the store should be checked the same way a spray is.

3. **Restricted use pesticides need a dealer permit.** "It shall be unlawful for any person to sell, offer for sale, or distribute within this state any restricted use pesticide without first obtaining a restricted use pesticide dealer's permit issued by the Board." The fee is "Fifty Dollars ($50.00) for each location" per year. Nothing in the product lists below is a restricted use pesticide, and an eco store has no reason to carry one, so the simple answer is to stay entirely out of restricted use products and avoid needing the permit at all.

**The practical action:** before listing any pest control product, look it up in Oklahoma's registered product database. ODAFF publishes registered products through Kelly Registration Systems at [kellysolutions.com/ok](https://www.kellysolutions.com/ok/pesticideindex.htm), and there is a newer [ODAFF Online pesticide product search](https://accessodaff.ag.ok.gov/share/YQCkhPWhwK/pesticide/pesticide-product-search). Both need to be opened in a normal browser: the Kelly site refused automated requests during this research and the ODAFF Online search is a JavaScript application, so neither could be queried here. Checking a dozen products by hand is an afternoon of work, not a project.

Sources: [Oklahoma Combined Pesticide Law and Rules, ODAFF](https://ag.ok.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Oklahoma-Combined-Pesticide-Law-and-Rules.pdf) (quotations above are from the text of that document), [ODAFF restricted use pesticide dealer permit application](https://ag.ok.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/ODAFF-Restricted-Use-Pesticide-Dealer-License.pdf), [EPA minimum risk pesticides](https://www.epa.gov/minimum-risk-pesticides/minimum-risk-pesticide-definition-and-product-confirmation), [ODAFF licensing and permits](https://ag.ok.gov/licensing-permits/).

#### 1.3 Fertilizers and soil amendments: a second registration regime

This was not in the brief and it should have been, because it bites the "organic fertilizers and amendments" category directly.

**Fertilizer.** Under the Oklahoma Fertilizer Act, "Any person engaged in the distribution or sale of fertilizer shall obtain a license", at fifty dollars a year per business location. Products in packages of less than thirty pounds are "specialty fertilizer", and "Registrations for specialty fertilizer products sold in packages of less than thirty (30) pounds shall pay a one-hundred-dollar registration fee for each product", expiring every June 30. Almost everything an eco store would sell is under thirty pounds, so almost everything is specialty fertilizer at $100 per product per year.

**Two exemptions rescue the retailer, and they are worth reading carefully:**

- On product registration: "A distributor shall not be required to register any fertilizer which is already registered under the Oklahoma Fertilizer Act by another person, provided the label does not differ in any respect." So if the brand has registered the product in Oklahoma, the store does not pay $100 again. The condition is that the label is unchanged, which is another argument against private labelling.
- On the licence: the Act's definition of "fertilizer dealer" excludes "a retail store selling only bagged registered commercial fertilizer other than bagged ammonium nitrate". A plain retail store selling other people's already registered bagged product is outside the definition.

**The honest caveat:** that second exemption says "commercial fertilizer", while the under thirty pound goods are defined as "specialty fertilizer". Whether the retail store exclusion covers a store selling only specialty fertilizer, and whether a bottle of liquid fish emulsion counts as "bagged", are exactly the kind of questions that should be put to ODAFF Plant Industry in one phone call rather than guessed at. Do that before the fertilizer category goes live.

**Soil amendments are a separate registration again.** ODAFF requires every soil amendment product to be registered before sale or distribution in Oklahoma, at "$100.00 per product", expiring December 31 each year, and the application demands the label, all advertising material, and "proof of claims" backed by university or independent research. That burden is clearly aimed at the manufacturer, not a shop, but the category is the one that covers worm castings, compost, mycorrhizal inoculants, humic acid and similar. Confirm with ODAFF that the reseller position mirrors the fertilizer one.

Sources: [Oklahoma Fertilizer Act and Rules, ODAFF](https://ag.ok.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Oklahoma-Fertilizer-Act-Rules.pdf), [OAC 35:30-29-51 fertilizer license and fees](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oklahoma/OAC-35-30-29-51), [ODAFF soil amendment registration form and directions](https://ag.ok.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Soil-Amendment-Registration-Form-1.pdf), [ODAFF Plant Industry](https://ag.ok.gov/plant-industry/).

#### 1.4 Personal care: MoCRA, and the private label trap

Under the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act, facilities that manufacture or process cosmetics must register with FDA, and the "responsible person", defined as the manufacturer, packer or distributor whose name appears on the product label, must list each product, hold safety substantiation records and handle adverse event reports.

**As a pure retailer, Lavish Leaf has no MoCRA registration duty.** Labelers, packagers, holders, distributors and retailers do not register. The moment the company puts its own name on a shampoo bar or a deodorant, it becomes the responsible person and inherits the whole set of obligations. That is the single strongest argument for selling other people's brands at launch and leaving own label for later.

Two product specific notes for the personal care table:

- **Toothpaste and toothpaste tablets containing fluoride are OTC drugs**, not cosmetics, under the anticaries monograph at 21 CFR 355. Fluoride free versions that only claim cleaning are cosmetics. Selling them is fine, but the labelling burden sits with a manufacturer who understands the monograph, which again argues against private label.
- **Deodorant is a cosmetic, antiperspirant is an OTC drug.** This distinction is well established but was not separately source verified in this pass, so confirm before writing any category copy that makes a claim.

Sources: [FDA MoCRA registration and listing overview](https://www.spakinect.com/news/fda-mocra-registration-product-listing-cosmetics), [How MoCRA is reshaping FDA oversight, Foley and Lardner, March 2026](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/how-mocra-is-reshaping-fda-oversight-of-cosmetics-in-2026/), [OTC Monograph M021, anticaries drug products](https://dps-admin.fda.gov/omuf/sites/omuf/files/monograph-documents/2023-05/OTC%20Monograph%20M021-Anticaries%20Drug%20Products%20for%20OTC%20Human%20Use%2005.02.2023.pdf).

#### 1.5 Green claims: what to check, and what not to print

The FTC Green Guides are still the 2012 edition. The FTC asked for comment on an update in 2022 and, as of late 2025, had not issued one, so the old rules govern. Under them an unqualified "degradable" claim requires complete decomposition within a reasonably short period after customary disposal, and "compostable" must be qualified if the product will not break down in a home compost pile or if facilities are not available to most consumers. Selling a product is not the same as repeating its marketing, and the store's own category copy is the store's own liability.

The certifications worth checking, and what they actually mean:

| Mark | What it certifies | Where to verify |
|---|---|---|
| USDA Organic | The NOP standard, applied to agricultural outputs such as food and fibre. Most garden inputs are not eligible for it, so seeing it on a fertilizer bag is usually wrong or refers to an ingredient | [USDA Organic INTEGRITY database](https://organic.ams.usda.gov/integrity/) |
| OMRI Listed | The right mark for inputs. OMRI reviews fertilizers, amendments and pesticides for allowed use in certified organic production. This is the one that matters for the garden category | [OMRI product search](https://www.omri.org/omri-search) |
| EPA registration | A pesticide has been reviewed and carries an EPA registration number. Necessary but not sufficient: Oklahoma registration is separate, see 1.2 | EPA reg number on the label, then the ODAFF lookup |
| Leaping Bunny | No animal testing across the whole supply chain, independently monitored, annual recommitment. "Cruelty free" on its own has no legal meaning in the US | [Leaping Bunny brand directory](https://www.leapingbunny.org/) |
| B Corp | A company level certification, not a product one. A brand claiming it and absent from the directory is a red flag | [B Corp directory](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/) |
| BPI Compostable | Industrial composting only. There is no US equivalent of home compostable certification, so a BPI mark does not mean it will rot in a backyard pile | [BPI certified products](https://bpiworld.org/) |
| TUV OK compost HOME | The genuine home compostable mark, European scheme. Rare on US products and worth a premium claim when present | TUV Austria certificate number |

The practical greenwashing rule for this store: a product is only as green as its weakest claim. "Bamboo" with no FSC chain of custody, "biodegradable" with no standard named, and "compostable" without saying home or industrial are the three phrases that should never appear on a Lavish Leaf product page without a certificate behind them.

Sources: [FTC summary of the Green Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/environmental-claims-summary-green-guides), [Green Guides update still pending, Packaging Dive](https://www.packagingdive.com/news/packaging-labeling-recyclable-compostable-green-guides/738514/), [OMRI lists and standards](https://www.omri.org/lists-and-standards), [BPI versus OK compost home](https://holdonbags.com/pages/tuv-bpi-composting-certifications-explained).

#### 1.6 The 30 day rule, which dropshipping breaks quietly

The FTC's Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule applies to this store and it applies to dropshippers specifically. The requirements, in short:

- Ship within the time advertised. If no time is advertised, ship within 30 days.
- The seller must have a reasonable basis for the time it promises.
- If shipment will be late, notify the customer no later than the originally promised ship date and offer a choice: consent to the delay, or cancel and get a prompt refund.
- Silence can be treated as consent for a first delay of up to 30 days. Longer delays, indefinite delays, and any second delay need actual consent. Without it the money must be refunded without the customer having to ask.

This is the rule that makes long haul dropshipping genuinely risky rather than merely annoying. A product coming from a warehouse in China on a slow economy line, now going through full customs processing, can miss 30 days without anybody doing anything wrong. The store, not the supplier, is the one on the hook. Two defences: publish a realistic shipping window on every product page rather than leaving it blank, and prefer suppliers shipping from US warehouses for anything the store promises quickly.

Source: [FTC, selling on the internet, prompt delivery rules](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/selling-internet-prompt-delivery-rules), [FTC Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule).

#### 1.7 Imports changed in 2025, and it matters for anything from China

The US de minimis exemption, the rule that let commercial parcels under $800 enter duty free with minimal processing, is gone. Executive Order 14324 suspended duty free de minimis treatment for all countries effective 12:01 a.m. Eastern on 2025-08-29. CBP then made the suspension indefinite through two interim final rules, effective 2026-06-24 for shipments outside the postal network and 2026-07-24 for mail.

The practical effect for a dropship store: every low value parcel arriving from abroad now owes applicable duties, taxes and fees, and goes through full customs processing. That adds cost and it adds days, on top of transit. Any margin table built on pre 2025 China dropshipping arithmetic is wrong. This is the single largest reason to treat the third supplier as a limited tool rather than a backbone.

Source: [Federal Register, notice implementing EO 14324 suspending duty free de minimis treatment for all countries](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/02/2025-16802/notice-of-implementation-of-the-presidents-executive-order-14324-suspending-duty-free-de-minimis), [Federal Register, indefinite suspension of the de minimis exemption for merchandise arriving through all modes other than the international postal network](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other), [CBP guidance CSMS 66065494](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/3f01456).

#### 1H. The pricing trap, and it is a serious one

Bangalla displays case prices publicly, which is unusual and looks like a gift. **Divide them out and they do not behave like wholesale prices.** Worked examples from the tables above:

| Product | Computed per unit from the public case price | Common US retail |
|---|---|---|
| Seventh Generation Free and Clear Ultra Liquid Laundry, 100 oz | $26.95 | roughly $18 |
| Seventh Generation Disinfecting Wipes, 35 ct | $8.70 | roughly $5 to $7 |
| Mrs. Meyer's dryer sheets, 80 count | $15.00 | roughly $7 to $10 |
| If You Care household gloves, one pair | $6.29 | roughly $5 to $7 |
| Bon Ami Polish and Cleanser, 14 oz | $3.42 | roughly $2.79 to $3.99 |

A wholesale price that sits at or above shelf price is not a wholesale price. Bangalla's own eco home page states that only approved accounts can access products and sub categories in that area, so the most likely explanation is that **logged out visitors are shown a list or reference price, and approved accounts see something lower.** A second possibility is that these are accurate case prices for a small distributor and the margins on mainstream brands really are thin, in which case the conclusion for the store is the same.

Either way the practical instruction is identical and it is important:

**Do not build any pricing model, margin table or product page from the prices visible on Bangalla's public pages.** Open the account, pull the real CSV and the daily stock file, and rebuild the numbers from those. This report deliberately does not publish a margin column for Bangalla, because computing one from observed public prices would produce numbers that look authoritative and are probably wrong.

What can be said with confidence, from Part 4.1: expect somewhere between 25 and 40 percent off retail through a distributor dropship arrangement, favour the concentrated and powdered formats where the shipping weight per use is lowest, and be realistic that mainstream brands sold in every grocery store will carry the thinnest margins in the catalogue. The specialist brands, Charlie's Soap, Bi-O-Kleen, Bio-Pac, BioBag, Earth Breeze, Bee's Wrap, are where both the margin and the differentiation are more likely to live.

## C. Arbico: the terms, the Oklahoma shipping bans, and the 2026-09-20 lines

Read before the distributor application is posted. **C includes the
state-by-state shipping restrictions**, which is the paragraph that changed
the garden list in §3.1.

**Verdict: usable, and better than expected, but not as a plain static storefront.** Arbico runs a genuine distributor dropship program with terms a small seller would struggle to beat. The problems are not commercial, they are regulatory and technical, and one of them is severe enough to change the website plan.

#### 2A. The terms

Arbico operates a formal **Distributor Program**, distinct from its affiliate scheme, and it is built around dropshipping. Source: [Arbico distributor program information](https://www.arbico-organics.com/category/distributor-program-information).

| Term | What the public page states |
|---|---|
| Dropship fee | None. The page says there are no additional dropship fees |
| Minimum order | None, no minimums stated |
| Case break fee | None |
| Blind shipping | Yes, shipped under your brand to your customer |
| Inventory required | None, including for live insects |
| How to apply | Download a distributor application PDF, return it by email to distributors@arbico.com or by fax |
| Sales channel restriction | Your own website, social media and privately managed channels only. **Amazon, Walmart, Alibaba and other third party marketplaces are explicitly prohibited** |
| Geography | US domestic addresses only |
| Discount tiers, annual minimum, resale certificate, MAP | **Not published.** These live in the application PDF, which was not publicly reachable |

Those four unpublished terms are the ones that decide whether this is a business. A resale certificate will almost certainly be required, because otherwise Arbico would have to charge sales tax. Everything else needs one email to distributors@arbico.com before any plan is built on it.

Two things to ignore: a coupon aggregator advertising "save up to 15 percent" refers to retail quantity breaks on the consumer site, not distributor pricing. And the separate [affiliate program](https://www.arbico-organics.com/category/affiliate-program) at 7.5 percent commission is closed to new applicants.

#### 2B. Integration: there is none

No product feed, no CSV export, no API, no platform app. Nothing in the distributor page, the affiliate page or anywhere else on the site. The only bulk product data Arbico publishes is a seasonal PDF catalogue, most recently Spring 2026, in an [archive going back to 2018](https://www.arbico-organics.com/category/catalog-archive).

Every SKU is hand keyed: name, size variants, description, price, image, and a per SKU state restriction list. Budget ten to twenty minutes per SKU to do it properly, so a fifty SKU catalogue is a ten to fifteen hour build. That is tolerable. Three things that follow from it are not:

- **Prices move and a static page goes stale silently.** During this research a site wide "Fall Beneficial Nematode Sale, up to 30 percent off" was running from 2026-08-28 to 2026-09-30, so every nematode carries a list price and a sale price, and those sale prices expire in ten days.
- **Stock status is invisible.** At read time Arbico's wild collected ladybugs, Trichogramma pretiosum, Alfalfa Meal 4 lb, Glacial Rock Dust, Volcanic Rock Dust and both Impact Organics castings sizes were out of stock, and praying mantid egg cases cannot ship at all in 2026 because of an APHIS restriction stated on their own page. A static page will happily sell every one of them.
- **There is no order handoff.** Every order becomes a manual re-key into Arbico's system or a phone call.

#### 2C. The finding that changes the website plan

Arbico applies **per SKU state shipping bans**, and they are extensive. These are not hazmat rules. Arbico explains them plainly: the manufacturer chose not to register that product in that state, usually because the annual state fee is not worth it for the volume. Source: [why some products have shipping restrictions](https://www.arbico-organics.com/why_restrictions). This is the same state registration regime described in Part 1.2 and 1.3, viewed from the supplier's side.

Verified examples as of 2026-09-20:

| Product | Cannot ship to |
|---|---|
| Fertilome Come And Get It II Fire Ant Killer | 33 jurisdictions including CA, NY, IL, PA, OH, MI, WA |
| Down To Earth Garden Gypsum | 26 jurisdictions **including Oklahoma** |
| Perma-Guard Crawling Insect DE | 19 states |
| Down To Earth Granular Humic Acids | 15 jurisdictions **including Oklahoma** |
| Monterey Insecticidal Soap RTU | AK, DC, HI, ME, MT, ND, SC, SD, WY |
| Down To Earth All Purpose 4-6-2 | IA, ND, **Oklahoma**, WV, WY |
| Actinovate SP | DC, AZ, PR |
| Monterey 70 percent Neem Oil RTU | WY |

**Three of those cannot be shipped into Oklahoma at all**, which means Lavish Leaf could not sell them to a customer in its own home state. That is the fertilizer and soil amendment registration regime from Part 1.3 biting in practice, and it is direct evidence that the regime is enforced rather than theoretical.

There is no honest way to express this on a static HTML page. A per SKU, per state rule has to be enforced at checkout, or the store will take orders it cannot fill and then have to cancel them. This is the strongest argument in the whole report for finishing the existing Netlify Functions cart rather than hand coding product pages: the restriction logic belongs in the function.

#### 2D. Shipping

Source: [Arbico shipping information](https://www.arbico-organics.com/category/shipping-info).

- **Origin:** Oro Valley, Arizona, in the Tucson metro.
- **Carriers:** USPS, FedEx and UPS. FedEx and UPS to physical addresses only, USPS for PO boxes.
- **Processing:** 24 to 48 hours excluding weekends.
- **Transit to Oklahoma:** **ESTIMATE, two to three business days by ground.** Arbico publishes no zone map or transit chart, so this is inferred from standard ground zones, not their figure.
- **Free shipping** is a per product flag, not an order value threshold. There are separate free ground, free two day and free overnight tiers attached to individual products, lower 48 only. Source: [free shipping category](https://www.arbico-organics.com/category/free-shipping).
- **Surcharges:** $7.50 for signature required, $20 to upgrade a free shipping item to overnight, and orders over 150 lb become freight requiring a quote.
- **Who pays:** the customer, unless the specific product carries a free shipping flag.

**Live insects run on their own timetable**, and this is the hardest part of the catalogue to operate:

| Regime | Products | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Tuesday only, overnight | Green lacewing larvae, Stratiolaelaps, Aphidius colemani, Amblyseius cucumeris, two spotted ladybugs | FedEx Priority Overnight, ships Tuesdays only, six to seven day lead time, Tuesday noon cutoff, must be used within 4 to 18 hours of receipt |
| Multi day, ground or two day | Lacewing eggs and cards, nematodes, Encarsia formosa, Fly Eliminators | Monday to Thursday or Monday to Friday, often free USPS or second day air |

Nematodes have a fourteen day refrigerated shelf life and arrive with a frozen ice pack. Cancelling or modifying a scheduled live shipment needs seven business days notice. Nearly every live product excludes HI, PR, VI, GU, AS, PW and AK.

The [live shipment guarantee](https://www.arbico-organics.com/category/live-shipment-guarantee) promises live arrival and a resend, but requires a report within 24 hours with photos or video, requires that the product not be discarded, and **excludes shipments left unattended at the delivery address**. In a dropship model the store has no control over a customer's porch, so that exclusion is the largest live goods liability in the catalogue.

#### 2E. Returns

Source: [Arbico return policy](https://www.arbico-organics.com/category/return-policy).

- 30 days from purchase, unused, unopened, undamaged, original packaging only.
- **20 percent restocking fee** on items that shipped with free shipping.
- Customer pays return shipping. Original shipping is not refundable. Refused packages have freight both ways deducted.
- RMA required in advance from Customer Care.
- **No returns on live product, ever.** Only the 24 hour viability claim.
- Opened pesticides are not returnable, which is normal and legally sensible.
- Clearance, closeout and discontinued items are final sale.

Arbico handles the RMA, but in a blind dropship model the customer contacts the store first, so the store absorbs the coordination. Note the arithmetic: a returned free shipping item costs the 20 percent restocking fee plus the original outbound freight plus the customer's return freight. On a typical garden margin that is a net loss on any return, which is an argument for a generous but carefully worded returns page rather than a permissive one.

#### 2F. Other blockers

- **Marketplace prohibition.** No Amazon, no Walmart. If growth was ever going to run through marketplaces, this supplier forecloses it.
- **Freight and dimensional weight.** The Compost Wizard Dueling Tumbler and Soil Machine PRO are oversize, ground only, no expedited option, and ship assembled. Tank's Organic Compost requires a phone order and a manual shipping quote. Rice hulls and coir blocks are dimensional weight exposure. See Part 4.3.
- **Hazmat** is a low concern here. No explicit hazmat or ORM-D restrictions were found, and ORM-D was phased out by USDOT in 2021. Surround WP is flagged ground only, which is the nearest thing to a hazmat signal on the site.
- **MAP policy is unpublished.** Ask before setting any price.

#### 2G. What the certifications on these pages actually say

Worth reading carefully, because Arbico uses "OMRI Listed" heavily in general marketing copy while individual product pages differ.

- For nearly everything Arbico sells, **OMRI Listed is the correct claim and USDA Organic is not**. Writing "USDA Organic" on a bottle of neem oil would be a false claim. See Part 1.5.
- Two adjacent marks appear: **CDFA Registered Organic Input Material**, California's state input programme, on most Down To Earth products, and **WSDA Approved**, Washington's, on Entrust SC and Surround WP. Both are legitimate input programmes. Neither is USDA Organic.
- Some pages say "NOP compliant", which is a statement about use in organic systems, not a certification. Reproduce that wording verbatim or not at all.
- **Copy the claim only from the exact SKU page.** Several products, including Bonide Liquid Copper, Bonide Sulfur, Mosquito Dunks and Come And Get It II, carried no OMRI badge on their own page despite the site wide copy.
- Fly Eliminators says "USDA approved". That is neither USDA Organic nor an EPA registration. Do not upgrade it.
- **Arbico does not print EPA registration numbers in page text**, only in linked manufacturer label PDFs. So in the tables below, EPA registration is recorded as "not stated on page" throughout, and any note about a product being a registered pesticide is an inference from the active ingredient. That is an absence of page text, not evidence either way.

- **Refills:** concentrate bottles, tablets, powders or pouches. Retail $5–15 each, ship for almost nothing. This is the profit.
- **Bulk refills:** gallon jugs and 5 lb pouches for the customer who is already converted. Lower margin per ounce, much larger basket.

### Things that replace plastic in a home

Recycle and storage bins, which he asked for, and the rest of the obvious set: **beeswax wraps**, **stainless or silicone food storage**, **glass jars with bamboo lids**, **produce bags**, **Swedish dishcloths** (one replaces ~15 rolls of paper towel and is the best margin-per-gram item in the store), **bamboo dish brushes with replaceable heads** (the head is the repeat sale), **wool or coconut scrubbers**, **compostable bin liners**, **stainless straws**, **reusable coffee filters**.

## H. What the money looks like

This section is independent of any one supplier and exists because the product tables below quote margins that look better than they are. A margin column is gross margin on the product only. It is not profit.

#### 4.1 The margin ladder, and where a small store sits on it

In natural products the convention is keystone: wholesale is roughly half of the suggested retail price, giving the retailer a 50 percent gross margin. That is the number a brand quotes when it sells direct to a shop. A distributor sitting in the middle needs its own 20 to 30 percent, and it takes that out of the retailer's half, not the brand's. So a dropship retailer buying through a distributor typically sees 25 to 40 percent off retail, not 50. Any table in this report showing a clean 50 percent on a dropshipped item should be read as optimistic unless the wholesale price was actually observed.

Source: [Shopify wholesale pricing guide](https://www.shopify.com/blog/product-pricing-for-wholesale-and-retail), [US wholesale pricing, MSRP and MAP](https://www.lnh31.com/blog/us-wholesale-pricing-strategy-msrp-map).

#### 4.2 A worked example, so the shape is clear

Take a solid dish bar with a $12.00 retail price and an assumed $7.20 dropship cost, that is 40 percent off retail.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Customer pays for the product | $12.00 |
| Supplier cost | minus $7.20 |
| Supplier dropship shipping, small light item | minus $6.00 to $9.00 unless the customer pays it |
| Payment processing, roughly 2.9 percent plus 30 cents on a $12 order | minus $0.65 |
| Cart platform, Snipcart at 2 percent, or zero on the existing custom cart | minus $0.24 or $0.00 |
| **Left over, if the store absorbs shipping** | **negative** |
| **Left over, if the customer pays actual shipping** | **about $3.90** |

The lesson is not subtle. **A single low value item shipped on its own loses money.** Every viable version of this store depends on one of three things: the customer pays shipping, there is a free shipping threshold high enough to force a multi item basket, or the store holds a small amount of stock itself and ships its own parcels. For a first launch the honest answer is usually a stated flat shipping fee plus a free shipping threshold somewhere around $50 to $75.

The numbers above use general processing rates and an assumed cost. Treat the shape as reliable and the digits as illustrative.

#### 4.3 Weight is the category killer

Garden inputs are heavy, and heavy is where dropship economics collapse. A 20 pound parcel on UPS Ground runs roughly $31 to $57 depending on zone, and 25 to 40 pound parcels sit higher still. A $22 bag of amendment that costs $38 to ship is not a product, it is a liability. Two practical consequences for the garden category:

- Favour concentrates and small formats. A 16 ounce bottle of neem concentrate that makes many gallons is a far better dropship product than a 20 pound bag of meal fertilizer, both for margin and for the customer.
- If bulky amendments matter to the mission, sell them as local pickup at the farm rather than shipping them. The company already runs community farms and a compost pickup service, which is a genuine advantage no competitor can copy and which sidesteps the freight problem entirely.

Source: [UPS ground shipping rates overview](https://www.ordoro.com/ups-ground-shipping-rates), [UPS shipping costs and rates](https://www.ups.com/us/en/support/shipping-support/shipping-costs-rates).

#### 4.4 Market price anchors, for sanity checking the tables

Spot checks on 2026-09-20 to keep the retail columns honest: laundry detergent sheets sell around $13.98 for 60 sheets, roughly 23 cents a sheet, at the mainstream end. Premium direct to consumer eco brands price a 16 fluid ounce dish soap around $9.95 and hair care around $19.95. A store pricing a shampoo bar at $18 is inside the market. A store pricing a bamboo dish brush at $18 is not.

Source: [Cleancult laundry sheets at Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/c/brand/cleancult), [Public Goods dish soap](https://www.publicgoods.com/products/laundry-detergent-sheets), [laundry detergent cost per load comparison](https://refillwatch.org/pillars/laundry-detergent-cost-per-load-guide/).

## I. CJdropshipping: why its tables are not carried forward

The 09-20 research priced five CJ categories (garden hand tools, seed
starting, compost gear, rain and watering, household swaps) and the tables
are **deliberately not reproduced here**, because the route is closed and a
priced table invites somebody to use it. The reasons, kept because they are
the ones that would decide any future Chinese-parcel supplier:

* **The de minimis exemption ended for China in May 2025 and for every
  country on 29 August 2025.** A small parcel now carries **54% duty or a
  $100 flat fee** by post, roughly 30% by courier, plus **$4 to $25 per
  parcel** in entry, broker and bond fees. The margin those tables showed
  does not survive it.
* **There is no eco or certification story**, which is the whole premise of
  this shop.
* **A return cannot be passed through to a US customer** at a sane cost.
* **Transit is long**, which runs straight into the FTC thirty-day rule in
  A above.
* His own reading agrees, and is the reason this section exists: *"I can’t
  really tell with CJ dropshipping, their website isn’t very user
  friendly."*
