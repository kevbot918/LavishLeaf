Privacy Policy

What Symphonymph and lavishleaf.org collect, what they do not, and who can see what.

Last updated 13 September 2026. This policy covers the Symphonymph Android app and the website at lavishleaf.org, both published by Lavish Leaf Inc.

The short version

Symphonymph has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. What you listen to, what you save and what you search for stay on your phone. We cannot see any of it, because it is never sent to us.

That is not a promise about our intentions; it is a description of how the app is built. There is no server of ours for the app to send anything to.

What stays on your phone

All of it. Specifically: your library, playlists, collections and Liked list; every playback position and bookmark; your listening history and listening time; your subscriptions; your settings, filters and search history; and anything you have downloaded. It lives in a database inside the app's private storage, and it is removed when you uninstall the app.

Android's own automatic backup is switched off for this app, so none of it is copied to your Google account either. If you want a copy, the app has its own Backup and Restore, which writes a file to wherever you choose.

What leaves your phone, and why

Requests to the catalogs

Symphonymph does not host, copy or relay audio. When you play something, your phone connects directly to the library, archive, broadcaster or publisher that holds it — the Internet Archive, LibriVox, the Library of Congress, Podcast Index, Radio Browser, SomaFM, Audius, Wikimedia Commons and others. Those requests reach them, not us, and like any request to any website they carry your IP address and an identifying User-Agent line that names this app and its version. Each of those organizations has its own privacy policy, and we have no access to their logs.

We send an honest User-Agent rather than disguising the app, because several of these archives are run by volunteers and deserve to know who is asking.

Voice search

If you tap the microphone in the search bar, the app hands the audio to Android's own speech recognizer, which is a separate app on your phone made by your phone's manufacturer or by Google. Depending on your device and its settings, that app may send the audio to its own servers to turn it into text. Symphonymph never records, stores or transmits audio itself, and it receives only the resulting text. The microphone is requested the first time you tap it, never at startup, and the feature can simply be left alone.

If you send us feedback

The app has a way to send us a bug report or a suggestion. Nothing is sent unless you fill it in and press send. What you write is submitted to a form hosted on this website, together with the app version, your Android version and your device model — because a bug report without those is usually unanswerable. Giving an email address is optional and is used only to reply to you. It is never added to a mailing list.

Links you follow

Sharing a recording, opening a source's own page, searching for lyrics or opening a donation page hands the link to another app on your phone — your browser, your mail app, the system share sheet. From that point you are on that service, under its rules, and we are not told where anything went.

What the app never does

It contains no analytics library, no crash-reporting library, no advertising library and no advertising identifier. It does not build a profile, does not use cookies, does not fingerprint your device, and does not sell or share data with anybody — because it does not have any to sell. There is no sign-in, so there is no account for anything to be attached to.

Children

Symphonymph is not directed at children under 13. It is a door onto public archives and a directory of independent podcasts, which we neither own nor moderate, so some of what it can reach is intended for adults. An explicit-content filter is built in, and it is off by default, which means an adult has to decide. We do not knowingly collect anything from anyone, of any age.

Permissions the app asks for, and when

  • Internet and network state — to stream, and to tell a metered connection from Wi-Fi for automatic downloads.
  • Notifications — the playback notification is how you control the player from outside the app. Android 13 and later require your permission to show it.
  • Microphone — voice search only, asked the first time you tap the microphone.
  • Read audio files — only if you open “On this device” to play music already on your phone. The app reads those files and never moves, changes or deletes one.

The website

lavishleaf.org is hosted by Netlify, which keeps ordinary server logs. The site uses Google Analytics to count visits; if you would rather it did not, any browser setting or extension that blocks it will work, and nothing on the site depends on it. Forms on this site (contact, newsletter, artist submissions, app feedback) are delivered by Netlify Forms and reach a Lavish Leaf inbox. We use what you send only to answer you, and we do not sell or pass on your details.

Keeping and deleting

App data: uninstalling the app removes everything, because there is nowhere else it exists. Form submissions and email: kept while they are useful and deleted on request. Write to support@lavishleaf.org and we will delete what we hold about you.

Your rights

Where the GDPR, the UK GDPR or the CCPA apply to you, they give you rights to see, correct, delete and port the personal data an organization holds about you, and to object to its processing. For the app the honest answer is that we hold nothing to show you. For anything you have sent us through a form or by email, write to the address above and we will act on it.

Changes

If this policy changes we will update the date at the top. If a change ever means the app starts collecting something it does not collect today, that will be said plainly in the app itself, not only here.
